## Dissertation {#dissertation}

My dissertation, "The Politics of Climate Adaptation," examines whether institutional adaptation affects individuals' engagement in risk-sensitive behaviors such as migration, and whether adaptation policies influence candidate electability. Original survey experiments with over 9,800 respondents recruited via social media in Indonesia.

<p class="v3-note">Committee: Daniel Masterson (chair), Matto Mildenberger, Mark Buntaine</p>

<div class="v3-papers">
<div><span class="v3-papers__num">01</span><h3>Paper 1: Risk and Adaptation</h3><p>Survey experiment (N=3,038) testing whether exposure to information about public climate adaptation projects shifts willingness to take risks. Vignette experiment with 5 treatment arms vs. control. Adaptation treatment increases risk-sensitive behavior by 0.08 SD (p=0.016).</p><span class="v3-pill">Manuscript in progress</span></div>
<div><span class="v3-papers__num">02</span><h3>Paper 2: Climate Promises and Voter Choice</h3><p>Three embedded survey experiments (N=6,773): candidate conjoint, policy conjoint, and corruption vignette. Climate adaptation promises do not significantly increase candidate support overall, but disaster experience moderates the effect.</p><span class="v3-pill">Analysis in progress</span></div>
<div><span class="v3-papers__num">03</span><h3>Paper 3: Partisan Alignment and Climate Adaptation Allocation</h3><p>How does partisan alignment between local and national governments affect the allocation of climate adaptation resources? Uses subnational fiscal and administrative data from Indonesia.</p><span class="v3-pill">In progress</span></div>
</div>
